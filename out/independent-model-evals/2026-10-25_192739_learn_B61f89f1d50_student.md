# Scoring AI on hard exams: what two independent test results tell us, and what they don't

### Explainer

AI companies often announce how well their models do on tests. A test used to compare AI systems is called a benchmark: a fixed set of questions with an answer key, so different models can be scored the same way. Two benchmarks appear in this news. GPQA Diamond is a set of very hard science questions. FrontierMath is a set of very hard math problems, sorted into difficulty levels called tiers, and Tier 4 is harder than Tiers 1-3.

The scores come from Epoch AI, an organization that ran the tests itself. An independent evaluation means someone other than the model's maker runs the test. That matters because a company's own numbers can reflect choices that favor its product, such as which tests to show or how to set them up. Epoch AI reports that it ran GPQA Diamond on DeepSeek's model deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017_max on 2026-10-19 and got a mean score of 0.932 [1]. On FrontierMath it reports a mean of 0.712 on Tiers 1-3 and 0.463 on Tier 4 for the same model [1]. A score of 0.932 means about 93% of the questions were answered correctly.

Epoch AI also tested a model called Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B_xhigh. It reports a GPQA Diamond mean of 0.886 on 2026-10-15 [2]. On FrontierMath it reports 0.698 on Tiers 1-3 and 0.317 on Tier 4 [2]. A separate organization, Artificial Analysis, lists a similar GPQA Diamond result of 89.0% for a model it calls Muse Spark Open (Xhigh), attributed to Meta [3]. That result is undated and may use a different method, so it is consistent with Epoch's figure but not an exact repeat of it [3].

Each score comes with an error bar, the "±" number. It shows how much a score could vary by chance, for example because of which questions were sampled or how the model answered on a given run. DeepSeek's GPQA score is 0.932 ± 0.016, so a reasonable range is about 0.916 to 0.948 [1]. The Muse Spark score of 0.886 ± 0.019 gives a range of about 0.867 to 0.905 [2]. Those ranges do not overlap, which suggests a real gap on this test. On FrontierMath Tiers 1-3, the scores are 0.712 ± 0.027 for DeepSeek [1] and 0.698 ± 0.027 for Muse Spark [2]. Their ranges overlap heavily, so these results do not clearly show one model ahead. On Tier 4, the figures are 0.463 ± 0.079 for DeepSeek [1] and 0.317 ± 0.074 for Muse Spark [2]. Those ranges barely touch, so the evidence favors DeepSeek but less firmly than on GPQA.

Two cautions apply. First, for the DeepSeek results, the confirmation rests on Epoch AI's own published evaluation, with no second source for these specific figures [1]. The same is true of the Muse Spark FrontierMath results [2]. Second, the models were tested on different dates [1][2], and a benchmark score describes performance on one test, not overall ability. Good readers ask who ran the test, whether anyone else repeated it, and how large the error bars are.
