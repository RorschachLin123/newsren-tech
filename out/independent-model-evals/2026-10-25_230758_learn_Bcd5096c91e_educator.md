### Summary for teacher

Epoch AI reports that Z.ai's GLM-5.3 is the top open-weight model on its Cyber ECI, a cybersecurity-focused version of its Epoch Capabilities Index, with a score of 160 [1]. That score places it above OpenAI's GPT-5.4, released in March 2026, though still below Claude Mythos Preview and GPT-5.5, released in April 2026 [1]. Epoch summarizes the situation by saying the open-weight frontier sits roughly where the closed-weight frontier stood 6–7 months earlier [1].

This is a useful case for teaching what benchmarks and evaluations actually measure. A benchmark is a standardized test—a fixed set of tasks with a scoring rule—that lets you compare systems on the same footing; an index like Epoch's ECI aggregates performance across evaluations into a single comparable number. 'Open-weight' means the trained model parameters are released so others can download and run them, as opposed to 'closed-weight' models accessed only through a developer's service. The claim here is not that GLM-5.3 beats every model, but that the best openly available model has reached a level that closed models occupied several months ago—a claim about a gap narrowing over time, not about who is currently best overall.

Use the scoring details carefully. A '±' after a score denotes one standard error; the interval score ± 1 SE captures roughly 68% of sampling variation, and a rough 95% interval is about two standard errors either side. Even when ranges overlap, two models are not clearly separated; a gap is only clear if it exceeds about two times the combined error, sqrt(a² + b²). Also stress the difference between an independent evaluation, like Epoch's own testing, and a developer's self-reported numbers: independent testing applies the same tasks and scoring consistently across competing models, whereas developers choose their own prompts and evaluations, which can favor their products.

### Misconceptions to watch

- Thinking 'top open-weight model' means 'best model overall'—the claim ranks GLM-5.3 only among open-weight models and places it below Claude Mythos Preview and GPT-5.5 [1].
- Treating score ± one standard error as the likely range for the true value; it covers only about 68%, and a rough 95% range is about two standard errors either side.
- Declaring one model ahead whenever ± ranges do not overlap; a gap is clear only if it is larger than about two times the combined error, sqrt(a² + b²).
- Assuming a benchmark score measures broad real-world ability; an index measures performance on standardized tasks—here a cybersecurity-focused version of the ECI—not every practical use [1].
- Equating a developer's self-reported numbers with independent testing; Epoch's figure comes from its own evaluation, which applies the same tasks and scoring across models [1].
- Reading a '6–7 month lag' as a permanent or precise gap; it is an approximate comparison of frontier positions at two points in time [1].
