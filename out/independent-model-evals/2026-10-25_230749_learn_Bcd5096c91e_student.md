A company called Z.ai has a model called GLM-5.3, and an independent research group called Epoch AI says it is the highest-scoring open-weight model on a benchmark called the Cyber ECI [1]. A benchmark is a standardised test used to compare systems; here the test is a cybersecurity-focused version of Epoch's wider Capabilities Index, which turns performance on many tasks into a single score [1]. "Open-weight" means the model's parameters are released so others can download and run it, as opposed to "closed-weight" models that you can only use through the developer's service. [1]

What makes the result interesting is where it sits. GLM-5.3 scored 160, which Epoch says is above OpenAI's GPT-5.4, released in March 2026 [1]. It scored below two closed models, Claude Mythos Preview and GPT-5.5, which came out in April 2026 [1]. Epoch's summary is that the best open model is roughly where the closed frontier stood 6–7 months earlier [1] — a rough way of saying the gap between freely available models and the very best proprietary ones is measured in months, not years. [1]

An independent evaluation matters because a developer's own numbers are produced by the people selling the product: they choose the tests, the prompts and what to publish, so flattering results are easy to select. Epoch runs its own tests and reports the result itself, which is why this claim is labelled independently verified [1]. Independent does not mean infallible — the same test design is still one choice among many — but it removes the conflict of interest that comes with self-reporting. [1]

One caution for reading any benchmark table: scores are estimates, not certainties. A "±" after a score is one standard error, and it describes how much the score would wobble if the test were repeated. One standard error either side covers only about 68% of likely values; a rough 95% range is about two standard errors either side. Also, two models should not be called clearly separated just because their ± ranges do not overlap. A gap is clear only if it is larger than about two times the combined error, computed as the square root of the sum of the squares of the two standard errors. The verified material here gives GLM-5.3's score but not the error bars for it or its rivals, so the ordering should be read as Epoch's point estimate rather than a proven separation. [1]

### Glossary

- **Open-weight model**: A model whose internal parameters are released so others can download and run it, unlike a closed-weight model available only through its developer's service.
- **Benchmark**: A standardised test or collection of tasks used to compare how well different systems perform, usually summarised as a score.
- **Capabilities Index (ECI)**: A single number that aggregates a model's performance across many tasks; a higher score means stronger measured capability on those tasks.
- **Cyber ECI**: A cybersecurity-focused version of the Epoch Capabilities Index, used to score models on security-related tasks.
- **Independent evaluation**: Testing carried out by someone other than the developer, reducing the conflict of interest that comes with self-reported results.
- **Standard error**: A measure of how much a score would likely change if the test were repeated; written as ± after a score.
- **95% range**: A rough interval covering about 95% of likely values; for a score it is about two standard errors either side.
- **Clear separation**: When a gap between two scores is larger than about two times the combined error, sqrt(a² + b²), for the two scores; otherwise the results do not clearly separate the models.
- **Closed-weight frontier**: The best-performing models that are not publicly released as weights, representing the leading edge of proprietary systems at a given time.
